## The MVP Feature Set

Here's exactly what's live and what's shipping next:

### Always-On Background Service

The first thing I had to solve was survival. Android kills apps the moment they go to the background. A driver assistant that dies when you minimize it is useless.

Jack runs as an **Android foreground service** — the same pattern Spotify, Google Maps, and Waze use to stay alive. A persistent notification tells the OS: *this one stays*. Jack earns its place in the system tray and it never lets go.

### "Hey Jack" — Wake Word Activation

You don't open the app. You don't tap a button. You say **"Hey Jack"** and the assistant wakes up, ready for your next command. Hands on the wheel. Eyes on the road.

The voice state machine runs through five states in sequence:

- **Listening** — always-on passive mode, waiting for the wake word
- **Command** — wake word received, listening for instruction
- **Processing** — categorizing the input
- **Destination** — confirming where you want to go
- **Idle** — session complete, back to passive listening

### Community Road Reports

When you say *"Hey Jack, police ahead"* — that event is categorized, timestamped, tagged with your GPS coordinates, and pushed to **Firebase Firestore** in under 2 seconds. Every Jack user on the same road sees it immediately.

Four categories in v0.1.1, recognized by natural language keywords:

| | Say any of these | Jack broadcasts |
|:---:|---|---|
| 🚔 | police · cop · officer · radar | Police checkpoint — visible to all nearby drivers |
| 💥 | accident · crash · collision · wreck | Accident on the road — tagged with your GPS |
| ⚠️ | hazard · pothole · debris · obstacle | Road hazard ahead — exact location pinned |
| 🚦 | traffic · jam · slow · congestion | Congestion zone — duration tracked until expiry |

Every report auto-expires after **120 minutes**. No stale data. No manual cleanup. The community map is always current.

### GPS Proximity Alerts

Every 10 seconds — every 20 meters — Jack checks your position against active community reports. When you close in on one, Jack speaks:

- **500 m** — first heads up
- **300 m** — closing in warning
- **200 m** — final alert before you're on it

You knew before you got there. That's the point.

### Navigation Relay

Jack intercepts turn-by-turn notifications from **Google Maps** and **Waze** via Android's `NotificationListenerService` and reads them aloud through the speaker. You don't switch apps. You don't glance at a banner. Jack handles it.

Your nav app of choice keeps working exactly as it always did. Jack just removes the screen from the equation.

### Bluetooth Auto-Activation *(shipping next)*

The friction of opening an app and tapping START goes to zero. When your phone pairs with your car's Bluetooth, Jack starts automatically — session open, listening active, ready to go. Arrive at the car, plug in, drive. Jack is already there.

This is handled via an Android `BroadcastReceiver` listening for `BluetoothDevice.ACTION_ACL_CONNECTED` — registered in the manifest, fires on connection, triggers the foreground service. No user interaction required.

### Onboarding — One Time, Two Steps

First launch: Jack asks your name and your preferred navigation app (Google Maps or Waze). That's it. Stored in `SharedPreferences`. Never asked again. Every trip after that: open the app, tap START, put the phone down.

## The Stack

Every technology decision was made against one constraint: **build fast, run permanently**.

- **Expo SDK 53 + React Native 0.79** — fast cross-platform scaffolding with a direct Kotlin escape hatch for the parts that need to be native
- **Kotlin** — the entire Android layer: `JackForegroundService`, `JackSpeechManager`, `JackNotificationListenerService`, `JackFirestoreManager`, `JackProximityChecker`
- **Firebase Firestore** — real-time document sync for community reports; no server to operate, no infrastructure to maintain
- **Android Foreground Service** — OS-guaranteed runtime; survives backgrounding, screen lock, and battery optimization
- **TypeScript** — strict typing across the JS/native boundary keeps the bridge honest

What's missing from this list is intentional. No custom backend. No database to run. No DevOps. The intelligence lives in the device and in Firestore. That's not a limitation — it's the architecture.

## The Roadmap

Jack at v0.1.1 is a proof of concept that runs on real hardware and solves real problems. Here's where it goes from here:

**Phase 3 — AI Brain**
Replace keyword matching with **GPT-4o intent recognition**. Natural language commands. *"Hey Jack, I need to stop for gas in about 10 minutes"* becomes an actionable request, not a confused silence. Deep **Google Maps API** integration so Jack can set your destination by voice and reroute mid-trip without you ever opening Maps.

**Phase 4 — Platform Expansion**
Bluetooth auto-activation ships. iOS + Apple CarPlay support arrives (Swift equivalent of the Kotlin native layer). Offline event caching for connectivity dead zones. Google Play Store launch with package `dev.js17.jack` — already production-ready in the manifest.

**Phase 5 — Monetization & Data**
Premium subscription tier with AI features, custom wake words, and trip history. Fleet management SaaS for logistics companies. Anonymized road event data licensing to municipalities and insurers. Insurance partnership program for usage-based discount integration.
